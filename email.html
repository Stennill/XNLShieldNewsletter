⚠️ This Week's Threat

The "Toll Road Fee" Text Scam

Also known as: Smishing (that's "SMS phishing" — fake texts trying to trick you)

📖 How This Scam Works

Picture this: You're going about your day when your phone buzzes with a text message. It looks official — maybe it says it's from "E-ZPass," "SunPass," "FasTrak," or your state's toll system. The message claims you have an unpaid toll fee, usually something small like $6.99 or $12.50.

Here's the scary part: The text warns that if you don't pay immediately, you'll face late fees of $50 or more, or even have your license suspended. There's a link to "pay now" and resolve the issue. That link is the trap.

When you tap that link, you land on a website that looks almost exactly like the real toll agency's site. It asks for your name, address, and — here's what they really want — your credit card number, expiration date, and security code. Some versions even ask for your driver's license number.

The moment you hit "submit," the scammers have everything they need to steal your money and your identity. They might charge your card immediately, sell your information to other criminals, or open new accounts in your name. Even people who don't use toll roads are getting these texts — that's how you know it's fake.

🔍 How To Spot This Scam

✓  The link looks weird. Real toll agencies use simple web addresses like "ezpassva.com" — scam links have random letters, numbers, or strange endings like ".top" or ".xyz" (example: "ezpass-payment-center.com.xyz123.top")
✓  It creates panic. Words like "URGENT," "IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED," or "YOUR LICENSE WILL BE SUSPENDED" are designed to scare you into acting before thinking
✓  You didn't use a toll road recently (or ever!) — yet somehow you "owe" money
✓  The phone number is unfamiliar. It might be a random 10-digit number or even an international number (starts with +1 but looks off)
✓  Small spelling mistakes or odd phrasing. Look for things like "Dear Customer" instead of your actual name, or sentences that sound slightly "off"

🛠️ What To Do If You Get One

1 DO NOT tap the link. Not even to "check if it's real." Just don't.

2 If you're worried it might be real, go directly to your toll agency's official website by typing the address yourself (like ezpassva.com) — or call the number on your actual toll statement.

3 Report the text. Forward it to 7726 (that spells "SPAM" on your keypad). This helps your phone carrier block these scammers.

4 Delete the message. Once reported, get it out of your phone so you're not tempted to second-guess yourself later.

😰 Already clicked and entered your info?

Don't panic, but act fast: Call your bank or credit card company RIGHT NOW and tell them your card may be compromised. They'll freeze it and send you a new one. Then, keep a close eye on your statements for the next few months.
